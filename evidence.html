How to interpret these sources

Regulatory law determines market requirements. Safety opinions inform risk assessments. Scientific studies inform possible mechanisms and effects under defined conditions. Marketing permission requires product-level compliant claims and labeling.

New concepts: nasal spray and D3 + K2 blister tablets

15 mL saline nasal spray: A nasal spray is not a food supplement. Its proposed intended use and principal mechanism determine whether medical-device or medicinal-product rules apply. Packaging, microbiological control and any claims require expert evaluation. BfArM classification guidance ↗.

30 blister-packed D3 + K2 tablets: A non-final supplement concept is being evaluated using a provisional adult daily target of vitamin D3 20 µg and K2 25 µg. The image displays different amounts (25 µg D3 + 75 µg K2); these are not verified label values. BfR discusses proposed recommended maximum contents and warns about vitamin K with anticoagulants.
