# Pharmed STAMINA and NO FLOW: herbal formulation revision

Prepared 14 October 2026 for formulation and manufacturing review.

STAMINA now combines two root extracts with CoQ10 and its existing micronutrients. NO FLOW now combines two botanical extracts with its existing amino acids, vitamin C and zinc. VIGOR's requested Tribulus revision is carried forward in the same website data files.

The amounts below are proposed development targets per planned daily serving. They require extract-specific toxicological review, manufacturer validation and destination-market assessment before final consumer directions or manufacture. Adding ingredients does not establish a stronger clinical effect of either finished blend.

## PHARMED STAMINA

| Ingredient | Proposed target per planned daily serving |
| --- | ---: |
| Rhodiola rosea root extract | 200 mg |
| Eleutherococcus senticosus root dry extract | 200 mg |
| Coenzyme Q10 | 100 mg |
| Magnesium, elemental | 150 mg |
| Vitamin C | 80 mg |
| Vitamin B1, thiamine | 1.1 mg |
| Vitamin B2, riboflavin | 1.4 mg |
| Vitamin B6 | 0.9 mg |
| Vitamin B12 | 2.5 µg |

Planned format: 60 tablets in blister strips and a carton. Two tablets form one planned daily serving, giving 30 servings per carton. The front label reads RHODIOLA + ELEUTHERO + CoQ10. Eleuthero is Eleutherococcus senticosus, a different species from Panax ginseng.

Rhodiola contributes the rosavin and salidroside marker profile. Eleutherococcus contributes a complementary root extract characterised by eleutherosides. CoQ10 participates in mitochondrial electron transfer; magnesium and the vitamins retain their normal nutritional functions. This is the ingredient rationale for the botanical energy profile.

### Extract specifications for development

| Material | Proposed specification and supplier information |
| --- | --- |
| Rhodiola | Authenticate Rhodiola rosea root. Target 3% rosavin and 1% salidroside, corresponding to 6 mg and 2 mg respectively at the 200 mg extract target. Confirm the named analytes, assay method, extraction solvent, native drug-extract ratio and carrier content. |
| Eleutherococcus | Authenticate Eleutherococcus senticosus root. Evaluate a native dry extract with a provisional drug-extract ratio of 13–20:1 and an ethanol extraction solvent in the 28–40% range. At 200 mg native extract this corresponds to 2.6–4.0 g starting root. Report eleutherosides B and E using a validated assay; agree acceptance limits with the supplier. |
| CoQ10 | Specify ubiquinone grade, assay and tablet-compatible carrier. Account for the mass of any dispersible preparation needed to deliver 100 mg CoQ10. |
| Magnesium | Specify the source salt and elemental assay. The 150 mg target is elemental magnesium, rather than the weight of the magnesium raw material. |

The eleutherococcus specification is an R&D selection within the preparation range described in the EMA herbal medicine monograph. It is not an approved dose or food status for this combination. If a supplied extract contains carriers, adjust its input mass to meet the agreed native-extract target and document the calculation.

The small De Bock exercise trial studied a particular 200 mg Rhodiola preparation; it does not validate this multi-ingredient product. NCCIH considers the overall evidence for Rhodiola insufficient for firm health conclusions. The EMA eleutherococcus conclusions are based on traditional use and explicitly exclude combinations. A randomized trial found no additional benefit from eleutherococcus added to stress-management training. These findings support careful ingredient characterisation, rather than a claim of proven stamina or ejaculation-control effects. [1–4]

The nominal sum of the listed STAMINA quantities is approximately 733.4 mg per planned serving. Actual tablet payload is higher because mineral salts, extract carriers, CoQ10 preparations, excipients and coating have their own mass. Validate compression, content uniformity, disintegration, stability and swallowability before confirming the two-tablet format.

Safety review should address the complete mixture and intended duration of use. Rhodiola and eleutherococcus can cause insomnia; eleutherococcus has also been associated with irritability, tachycardia and headache. NCCIH reports Rhodiola interaction concerns with losartan and CoQ10 interactions with warfarin and insulin. Evaluate medicines and relevant cardiovascular or metabolic conditions before finalising warnings and directions. [3–5]

## PHARMED NO FLOW

| Ingredient | Proposed target per planned daily serving |
| --- | ---: |
| L-citrulline | 3,000 mg |
| L-arginine | 500 mg |
| French maritime pine, Pinus pinaster, bark extract | 100 mg |
| Panax ginseng root extract | 200 mg |
| Vitamin C | 80 mg |
| Zinc, elemental | 5 mg |

Planned format: 120 tablets in blister strips and a carton. Four tablets form one planned daily serving, giving 30 servings per carton. The front label reads L-CITRULLINE + PINE BARK + GINSENG, with the descriptor AMINO + BOTANICAL COMPLEX.

The amino acids retain the nitric-oxide pathway rationale. Pine bark adds a procyanidin-rich polyphenol profile and Panax ginseng adds ginsenosides. Vitamin C and zinc retain their normal antioxidant nutritional roles.

### Extract specifications for development

| Material | Proposed specification and supplier information |
| --- | --- |
| Pine bark | Authenticate French maritime pine, Pinus pinaster, and the bark plant part. Evaluate a 65–75% procyanidin analytical target, with the assay method and reference standard explicitly stated. At 100 mg extract, the nominal marker target is 65–75 mg by that method. Record extraction solvent, native ratio and carriers. |
| Panax ginseng | Authenticate Panax ginseng root and document white/red processing. Evaluate 5% total ginsenosides as an analytical development target, equivalent to 10 mg at 200 mg extract. State the chromatographic marker profile, assay method, extraction ratio, solvent and carrier content. |
| Amino acids and zinc | Confirm identity and purity for both amino acids. Specify zinc salt and elemental assay. The 5 mg zinc target is elemental content. |

These proposed marker percentages are analytical targets, rather than proof of equivalence to a studied preparation. A generic pine bark extract must not be called Pycnogenol or Prelox, or assigned their trial results, without the relevant licensed material and exact formulation. Ginseng extract at 200 mg is not automatically equivalent to the red-ginseng preparations used in clinical studies.

Randomized trials have investigated specific combinations of pine bark extract and L-arginine aspartate. The proposed NO FLOW formula differs in materials, amino-acid composition and serving amounts. Their results therefore cannot establish efficacy of this blend. NCCIH reports some sexual-function findings for Asian ginseng but also limitations in the evidence. The EAU guideline discusses only modest potential effects of selected supplements in a limited clinical context. No promise of treating erectile dysfunction or achieving a stronger erection has been added. [6–9]

The nominal ingredient sum is 3,885 mg per planned serving, or 971.25 mg per tablet before accounting for zinc salt mass, carriers, excipients and coating. This is a demanding four-tablet payload. The manufacturer must demonstrate tablet size, compression, disintegration and swallowability, or revise the serving/tablet format openly. Do not lower the listed ingredient amounts merely to fit an unvalidated carton format.

Safety review should address blood-pressure medicines, anticoagulants/antiplatelets and diabetes medicines, with an interaction assessment for the complete mixture. Asian ginseng can cause insomnia, interfere with blood clotting and lower blood sugar. These products are separate formulations; this proposal does not establish the safety of taking several Pharmed men's products together. [8]

## VIGOR revision retained

| Ingredient | Target per planned daily serving |
| --- | ---: |
| Tribulus terrestris aerial-parts dry extract | 750 mg |
| Gelatinised maca root powder | 1,000 mg |
| Panax ginseng root extract | 200 mg |
| Zinc, elemental | 5 mg |
| Vitamin B1 | 1.1 mg |
| Vitamin B6 | 0.9 mg |

VIGOR remains a 120-tablet blister/carton proposal with four tablets per planned serving. Tribulus is included directly in its ingredient table and the separate optional-variant paragraph is removed. Its plant part, extract composition, native ratio, carrier content and toxicological profile still need specialist review, including reproductive safety and destination-market classification. Tribulus trials used different preparations and produced differing findings; the 750 mg target is not a claim of proven efficacy or increased testosterone. [10–12]

## Manufacturer review and artwork

For each botanical, obtain botanical identity testing, validated marker assays and a certificate of analysis covering microbial quality, heavy metals, pesticides and residual solvents. Establish appropriate acceptance limits for the destination market. Assess blend stability, tablet disintegration and blister moisture protection. Final labels must use the validated composition, directions and market-compliant claims.

The PNG cartons are presentation mockups, rather than printing dielines or released regulatory labels. The website uses ingredient-led descriptions and contains multilingual formulation targets and product information. The manufacturer brief provides the evidence boundaries and technical development requirements separately.
